§ The Method

Five devices, used relentlessly

How to read this book

This curriculum assumes nothing. Every term is defined the first time it is used, and every formula gets an analogy before it gets symbols. Five devices do all the work, and they repeat in the same order in every module — so once you have read one module, you already know how to read the other nine. There is no prose here that isn't one of these five things.

§ Sources

Three texts, plus the part no textbook covers

Intuition & financial-statement backbone

Desai — How Finance Works

The plain-English layer. Balance sheets as a snapshot and income statements as a video, cash flow versus profit, the time value of money as an idea before a formula, capital budgeting, and why a valuation is reasoned estimation rather than measurement. This book supplies the why you'd ever care half of every module.

Markets & portfolio backbone

Bodie, Kane & Marcus — Investments

The instruments and the portfolio. Equities and bonds as contracts, risk read as volatility rather than as “chance of ruin,” diversification, index funds, beta, market-cap versus equal weighting, and the behavioural finance that Module 9 closes on. This book supplies the what the market actually is layer.

Quantitative machinery

DeFusco, McLeavey, Pinto & Runkle — Quantitative Investment Analysis

The arithmetic done properly. Discounting and compounding, present and future value, net present value, real versus nominal rates, and scenario analysis as multiple internally consistent narratives rather than one point forecast. This book supplies the show your working layer, from Module 1's exponent to Module 7's DCF.

Signature control — the source-weight legend

Which text carries each topic — and where none of them do

Every topic tag in the curriculum below is coloured by its evidence. DESAI marks the intuition-and-statements layer; BODIE marks market and portfolio mechanics; QUANT marks material whose weight is the arithmetic itself. The fourth colour is the honest one: CANADA marks topics that none of the three texts covers, because they are jurisdictional — TFSA, RRSP and FHSA account placement, the Canada–US tax treaty's withholding exemption, the dividend tax credit, SEDAR+ filings, and the Canadian-listed all-in-one ETFs of Module 6.3. Those come from Canadian regulation and fund documentation, not from a textbook, and saying so is more useful than pretending otherwise. Filter by evidence — non-matching tags dim.

§ Curriculum

Ten modules, four phases, zero assumed knowledge

PHASE I Foundations Modules 00–02

The absolute basics, then the one formula the rest of the book is built on. Desai carries this phase almost entirely — net worth, statements, cash flow — with DeFusco supplying the time-value arithmetic and one Bank of Canada note that no textbook provides. Modules 01 and 02 open with an ECON/BUS refresher box rather than assuming the course content was retained cold.

MOD 00 The Absolute Basics

What money functionally is: assets, liabilities, and the one subtraction that tells the truth when a bank balance won't. Then interest, defined as the price of using someone else's money — which is literally what a bank is.

assets & liabilities net worth interest 4 definitions · 2 worked examples
MOD 01 ECON 201/202 Refresher, and the Time Value of Money

A dollar today is worth more than a dollar later, because today's dollar can be invested immediately. Present value, future value, and the exponent that makes a discount rate bite — plus compound interest, the real reason “start early” matters. Ships with a live TVM calculator.

time value of money present & future value compound interest opportunity cost Bank of Canada policy rate
MOD 02 BUS 100 (Ch. 17) Refresher: Your Personal Financial Statements

A balance sheet is a snapshot; an income statement is a video. Build both for yourself, read liquidity honestly, and see why a raise that expenses rise to match leaves cash flow exactly where it was.

balance sheet income statement cash flow liquid vs. illiquid assets
Phase exit

You can write your own balance sheet and one-month income statement from memory, state your net worth and monthly cash flow as two separate numbers, and explain out loud why the second matters month-to-month and the first year-to-year.

PHASE II Credit & Capital Decisions Modules 03–05

The same compounding arithmetic from Module 01, now running against you (credit) and then deployed deliberately (capital budgeting). Modules 03 and 04 each open with their BUS 100 refresher box. Module 05 has none — it starts from absolute zero on markets and needs no prior course to rebuild.

MOD 03 BUS 100 (Ch. 18) Refresher: Banking and Credit

Credit risk is priced directly into the rate you are charged — which is why a card rate and a mortgage rate differ by more than a decade of market conditions could explain. Secured versus unsecured, made concrete. Ships with a live amortization generator.

fractional reserve banking credit risk secured vs. unsecured pricing amortization
MOD 04 BUS 100 (Ch. 19) Refresher: Capital Budgeting for Your Own Life

Companies decide whether future cash from a project is worth more, today, than its cost now. So can you. Net present value, applied to a certification you are considering paying for — and an honest look at how completely the answer depends on your inputs. Ships with a live NPV scenario tool.

capital budgeting net present value discount rate sensitivity
MOD 05 Investment Markets, From Absolute Zero

A stock is a slice of a bakery — upside and downside both flow to you. A bond is lending the owner money, a contractual return regardless of how business goes. Then diversification, and the single product that delivers it in one purchase.

stocks & equity bonds, principal & coupons diversification index funds & ETFs
Phase exit

Given any borrowing or spending decision, you reach for a discount rate before an opinion — and you can say, without hedging, why paying down a 20% balance usually outranks investing the same dollar.

PHASE III Strategy & Institutional Analysis Modules 06–07

The two long modules, and the only place this book becomes jurisdictional. Bodie, Kane & Marcus carries the portfolio theory; the account-placement rules, the withholding-tax treatment and the Canadian-listed ETF line-up come from Canadian regulation and fund documentation instead, and are tagged as such. Module 07 runs gold mining as its worked example throughout, then reads a real ETF fact sheet end to end.

MOD 06 Building a Canadian Investment Strategy: ETFs as a Core, Stocks as a Satellite

Most of a portfolio's long-run outcome comes from structure, not from which stock got picked. Seven sections: why structure beats skill, the core–satellite split, the Canadian all-in-one ETF glide path, account placement, satellite sizing, rebalancing mechanics, and a full worked portfolio blueprint.

risk as volatility core–satellite strategy rebalancing all-in-one ETFs (VEQT/XEQT) TFSA / RRSP / FHSA placement withholding tax treaty dividend tax credit
MOD 07 Institutional-Grade Equity & Sector Analysis

From reading a single stock quote to a full sector research thesis, the way a junior equity research analyst would on day one. Twenty-six definitions, the four-pillar framework, a DCF, a practical filings checklist, and a real Global X PAVE fact sheet read line by line — including the tracking difference its expense ratio actually produced. Ships with a live bull/base/bear DCF sandbox.

market cap, EPS, P/E beta index weighting mechanics real interest rates scenario analysis discounted cash flow MD&A SEDAR+ filings
Phase exit

You can size a satellite sleeve before picking anything to put in it, run any candidate through all four pillars, and read an ETF fact sheet's sector breakdown to check whether its hundred holdings are real diversification or a hundred versions of one bet.

PHASE IV Macro & Discipline Modules 08–09

Two short modules, deliberately. Both close loops opened much earlier rather than introducing new machinery, and neither is padded out to match the length of the phase before it.

MOD 08 Macro Reconnection

The ECON 202 refresher, restated where it finally pays off: subtract inflation from a nominal rate and a comfortable-sounding savings account turns into roughly one percent real — which is why “just save” often underperforms investing over long horizons.

real vs. nominal rates inflation real return
MOD 09 Professional Discipline: Behavior and Ethics

Losses feel roughly twice as painful as equal gains feel good, which is what makes people sell at the exact worst moment. The consequence, tied straight back to Module 6.2: a portfolio you can psychologically hold beats a theoretically superior one you abandon.

loss aversion behavioural finance matching risk to horizon
The standing milestone

You hold your allocation through a real double-digit drawdown without changing it, because you chose it against a time horizon rather than against a mood — and you can say which module you would re-read if you ever felt the urge to change it mid-fall.

Where to start

Module 00 assumes nothing at all, and takes about fifteen minutes. If you already know what net worth means, start at Module 01 — every module after it leans on that one formula. The full Glossary of all forty-seven terms is available at any point.

Honest note on scope

Modules 08 and 09 are short, and are meant to be. Each closes a loop opened earlier — the real-rate identity from Module 01's inflation note, and the risk-tolerance argument from Module 6.2 — and neither has been padded out to look proportionate to Module 07. Length here tracks how much genuinely new machinery a module introduces, nothing else. Nothing on this page is financial advice; it is a curriculum.
