01  The Method

Five devices, used relentlessly

Nothing is assumed. Every term is defined the first time it is used, and every formula gets an analogy before it gets symbols. Five devices do all the work, and they repeat in the same order in every module — so once you have read one, you know how to read the other nine.

02  Sources

Three texts, plus the part no textbook covers

Intuition &
statements

Desai — How Finance Works

Balance sheets as a snapshot and income statements as a video, cash flow versus profit, the time value of money as an idea before a formula, capital budgeting, and why a valuation is reasoned estimation rather than measurement.

Markets &
portfolio

Bodie, Kane & Marcus — Investments

Equities and bonds as contracts, risk read as volatility rather than as the chance of ruin, diversification, index funds, beta, index weighting, and the behavioural finance the final module closes on.

Quantitative
machinery

DeFusco, McLeavey, Pinto & Runkle — Quantitative Investment Analysis

Discounting and compounding, present and future value, net present value, real versus nominal rates, and scenario analysis as several internally consistent narratives rather than one point forecast.

Which text carries each topic — and where none of them do

Every topic tag in the curriculum below is coloured by its evidence. The fourth colour is the honest one: Canada-specific marks topics none of the three texts cover, because they are jurisdictional — TFSA, RRSP and FHSA placement, the Canada–US treaty's withholding exemption, the dividend tax credit, SEDAR+ filings, and the Canadian-listed all-in-one ETFs of Module 6.3. Saying so is more useful than pretending otherwise. Filter by evidence; non-matching tags fade.

03  Curriculum

Ten modules, four phases, zero assumed knowledge

Read in order. Each module leans on the one before it, and the arc runs from a single subtraction to a full equity research thesis without ever introducing a second discounting formula.

The absolute basics, then the one formula the rest of the book is built on. Desai carries this phase almost entirely — net worth, statements, cash flow — with DeFusco supplying the time-value arithmetic. Modules 01 and 02 open with an ECON/BUS refresher rather than assuming the course content was retained cold.

Honest note on scope

Modules 08 and 09 are short, and are meant to be. Each closes a loop opened earlier — the real-rate identity from Module 01's inflation note, and the risk-tolerance argument from Module 6.2 — and neither has been padded out to look proportionate to Module 07. Length here tracks how much genuinely new machinery a module introduces, nothing else. Nothing on this page is financial advice; it is a curriculum.
